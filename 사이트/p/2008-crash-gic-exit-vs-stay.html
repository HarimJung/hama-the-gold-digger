The stay or switch table

Born in fearXIU2 min readSTEP 32026-11-06

TSX sellers in 2009 missed $24,293. Want it back?

In 2008 the TSX, Canada's main stock market, crashed. In an example from NEI, $10,000 put into a balanced portfolio — a mix of stocks and bonds — at the start of 2007 was worth only $7,530 by March 2009, the bottom. From there, money left in the mix grew to about $34,862 by 2025, while money moved into a 5-year GIC, a bank deposit with a fixed rate, grew to about $10,569, roughly the $10,000 it started with.

WHY IT MATTERSXIU is one fund that copies Canada's S&P/TSX 60 stock index.

It started trading in 1990, and iShares calls it the world's first ETF.

Because XIU holds the whole index and not one company, a crash like 2008 drags it down with the market and its recovery depends on the whole market coming back, which is the bet the NEI chart describes, though that chart tracked a balanced portfolio, not XIU, and left out fees and taxes.
